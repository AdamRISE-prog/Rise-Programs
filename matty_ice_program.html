Hypermobile Athlete — Read This: Mobility work for hypermobile athletes is different. The goal is NOT to gain more range — it's to build control and stability through the range you already have. Shortened stride, stacked positions, hip-led rotation under control. Do not chase end range. Build ownership of the range you have.
Flexible by Design: This program is a framework, not a mandate. Throw to comfort — if it doesn't feel right, back off the intent or distance. Everything is fluid. Communicate. The goal is to be ready for starts, not to hit arbitrary numbers.
Goals & Program Structure
Pitcher Goals
Throw harder — by shortening stride length and building rotational power through a more stable base
Gain stability — hypermobility means we control the range we have, not chase more of it
Control range of motion — every throw is a chance to practice a cleaner, more repeatable delivery
Be ready for starts — throw to comfort, build bullpen work, arrive at game readiness feeling good
Block Structure
Week 1: Light MWF — get the arm going, no pressure
Weeks 2–5: 4-day throw — build intensity, introduce bullpen
Week 6: Deload — light MWF, let the arm adapt
Weeks 7–8: Pulldown phase — 3 then 5 pulldowns
Week 9: Mound phase — pulldowns + bullpen off the mound
Program Focus Areas
01
Stride Control
Shortened stride = more hip stiffness = more velocity. Every throw: feel your back hip fully loaded before you land. The power is in the hip, not the reach.
02
Stability First
Hypermobile pitchers don't need more range — they need to own what they have. The lift and prep are designed to build stiffness and stability, not flexibility.
03
Arm Care
Arm care A on Tuesday, B on Friday — for strength. Higher weight, less volume. This is not maintenance — it's building the posterior cuff and scap to support the arm long-term.
01
Isometric Foundation
Weeks 1–5 · Week 1 MWF Light · Weeks 2–5 Four-Day Throw
Goal: Get the arm going in week 1, then build intensity across 4-day throw weeks. Bullpen work begins in week 2 and builds to 25 pitches by week 5. Lift follows isometric loading — position and tension are the stimulus. Arm Care A on Tuesday, B on Friday.

02
Eccentric / Volume + Pulldown Phase
Weeks 6–9 · Week 6 Deload MWF · Weeks 7–9 Pulldowns + Mound
Goal: Deload in week 6 — the arm adapts during rest. Pulldowns begin week 7 with 3 reps, build to 5 in week 8. Week 9 transitions to mound — bullpen off the hill and pulldowns on the same day. Lift shifts to eccentric loading — the descent is the stimulus.
